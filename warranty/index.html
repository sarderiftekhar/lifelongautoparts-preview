What we don't cover - and why we're telling you now

These are the exclusions that generate most of the complaints against this industry. You should know about them before you buy, not when you claim.

Freight on a warranty claim

Shipping the failed unit back and the replacement out. Ask us who pays this before you buy - it is the single most common surprise in this industry.

Electronics and bolt-on accessories

Switches, sensors, cables and wiring harnesses, plus bolt-ons such as brackets, manifolds and flywheels. They may ship with the unit, but they carry their own terms.

Wear items

Belts, hoses, water pumps and thermostats are maintenance parts, not warranty items - and fitting a new thermostat at installation is a condition of cover.

Overheating and lubrication failures

A melted heat tab or evidence of running low on oil voids the claim. Keep shop-generated service records - handwritten logs are not accepted.

Misuse and consequential damage

Accidents, racing, improper maintenance - and damage to other components from continuing to drive after the failure. This is why the activation steps matter.

Fitting by anyone unqualified

Installation has to be by a licensed repair facility, with the invoice retained. A self-installed unit, or unauthorised repair work, will not be covered.

About lifetime cover

Our name is LifeLong, so it is fair to ask. Lifetime cover on any part requires an annual inspection at an authorised centre, and we are not publishing a lifetime claim for the Western U.S. until we can confirm those centres exist in your state.
