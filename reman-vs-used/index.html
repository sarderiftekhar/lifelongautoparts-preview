Buyer's guide

Used or remanufactured?

The cheaper option is not automatically the wrong one, and the expensive one is not automatically the safe one. It depends on the vehicle and on how long you plan to keep it. Here is the comparison without the sales pitch.

Comparison of used and remanufactured units
  Used Remanufactured
What it is A complete assembly removed from a donor vehicle, tested and inspected. Stripped to the core, machined, and rebuilt with new wear components to a published tolerance.
Cost Lower, usually by a wide margin. Higher. The difference is often a large fraction of the total job.
What is new Nothing. It is an original assembly with its original wear. Bearings, seals, gaskets, rings and other wear parts. On a transmission, the clutches.
Mileage Recorded and disclosed. Varies unit to unit. Not applicable - wear components are new.
Known design faults Carried over. If the original had a weak point, so does this. Often addressed during the rebuild, which is why a good reman can outlast the factory unit.
Warranty Shorter. Longer, because more of the assembly is new.
Availability Depends what has come through. Rare trims can take time. More consistent on common applications, thinner on unusual ones.
Used

Choose used when

  • The vehicle has a defined amount of life left - you are keeping it two or three more years, not ten.
  • The repair has to fit a budget, and the alternative is not repairing it at all.
  • The rest of the drivetrain is tired too, so the extra life of a reman unit would outlast the car around it.
  • It is an older vehicle where a reman unit costs a meaningful fraction of what the vehicle is worth.
Remanufactured

Choose remanufactured when

  • You intend to keep the vehicle for years and want to stop thinking about it.
  • The vehicle is otherwise in good condition and worth investing in.
  • The original unit has a known design weakness that a rebuild corrects.
  • It is a working vehicle and downtime costs you money, so the longer coverage earns its price.

What about "rebuilt"?

The words are used loosely across this industry, which is worth knowing before you compare two quotes. Broadly: rebuilt usually means the unit was dismantled and the failed parts replaced, addressing the specific fault. Remanufactured usually means it was returned to a published specification, with all wear components replaced whether or not they had failed.

Nobody polices those definitions, so a rebuilt unit from one seller can be more thorough than a remanufactured one from another. Ask what was actually replaced. If a seller will not tell you, that is the answer.

The question that matters more than either

Whichever you choose, the warranty is what protects you if it goes wrong — and in this category the warranty that gets advertised and the warranty that gets paid are frequently different things. Before you buy from anyone, ask for three numbers: the labour reimbursement rate per hour, the total claim cap, and how many business days a claim takes.

Most sellers will not print those. Ours are on our warranty page.
